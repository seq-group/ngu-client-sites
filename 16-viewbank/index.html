The market

02

Nobody is refusing to buy. They are refusing to decide.

This is the most important thing I can tell you tonight, because it changes what a campaign actually has to do.

Source: Cotality Home Value Index, September 2026 release, results as at 31 August 2026. Bars drawn to a 0–100% scale.

Alongside that: national dwelling values are down for a fifth consecutive month and now sit 3.6% below the March peak. Regional Queensland is down 1.3% over the quarter. Cotality’s research director puts the cause plainly — elevated borrowing costs and serviceability limits, with higher-value housing falling fastest.

Here is what that does inside a buyer’s head. If they believe next month is cheaper, waiting is free. They are not being difficult; they are being rational. They inspect, they like it, they say they will have a think, and then nothing happens — because nothing is making anything happen.

Finance is the other half of it. Buyers are being approved for less and taking longer to get there, and a contract that depends on a bank is a contract that can collapse three weeks after you have told everyone the house is sold.

October is the busiest selling month of the year here, level with March and twice January. After it the market thins out and does not properly return until February. That is why the auction is dated 24 October and not later — the campaign runs into the deepest part of Beenleigh’s year, and finishes before it empties.

Source: Pricefinder peak selling periods for Beenleigh, median sales per month, from the CMA prepared 18 September 2026.

You cannot argue a buyer out of waiting.You can only put something in front of them that costs more than waiting does. There are exactly two things that qualify: another buyer, and a date. Everything in this plan exists to manufacture those two.

And this is the outcome I want to protect you from. A falling market punishes patience. Every month this sits unsold, the evidence underneath it gets weaker and the price starts looking like the problem. The house still advertised after Christmas is not the one that gets a premium — it is the one every buyer points to as proof that the price was wrong.

How a price is set

04

The quiet sale and the best sale are usually two different sales.

It is worth three minutes, because it is the reason this campaign ends at an auction rather than in a negotiation.

Think about how a price is actually arrived at. It is never set by the buyer who wants your house the most. That buyer stops the moment there is nobody behind them — they have no reason to go further, and they know it. The number lands where the second most determined buyer runs out of room. The winner pays one step past the runner-up, and not a dollar more.

The thing sellers usually picture when they hear “full campaign” is their own street — strangers on the driveway every Saturday, neighbours counting cars, the whole business conducted on the front lawn. It is worth knowing that is not where a 2026 campaign is loud. It is loud on phones. Look at what actually happened at number 23.

So the real choice is not quiet against loud. It is quiet-and-then-loud against quiet-and-then-slow, and the plan on the next page is built for the first one.

Why auction

06

At auction, you are the one who sets the terms.

This is the part of auction almost nobody explains properly to a seller — and in a market where buyers want conditions, it is the part that matters most.

In an ordinary private treaty sale, the buyer writes the terms and you respond. They pick the settlement date. They pick the deposit. They attach the conditions — finance, building and pest — that let them walk away. You sign, you tell your family it is sold, and then you wait three or four weeks to find out whether it actually is.

At auction it runs the other way. The contract is yours before a single bid is made. Every person who raises a hand has already read it, already done their building and pest, already sorted their money, and is agreeing to your terms — not negotiating them.

Read that against the market section and you will see why I keep coming back to it. The problem right now is not that buyers dislike houses. It is that they want a way out, and every way out they are given is a way your sale can fail. An auction says: you may have this house, on these terms, by this date. The buyers who cannot commit self-select out in week two — which is useful information early, rather than a nasty surprise in week six.

You are not just selling a house. You are selling it on conditions you chose.That is worth real money in a market where a finance clause is the most likely reason a sale collapses — and it is the one thing a quiet, private, one-buyer negotiation cannot give you.
